# The Sweetest Disaster: the Boston Molasses Flood (draft 1)

## Facts and sources
Sources: Wikipedia, "Great Molasses Flood"; Stephen Puleo, *Dark Tide* (2003); Smithsonian Magazine, "The Science of the Great Molasses Flood" (2016).
- 15 Jan 1919, about 12:30 pm, North End, Boston. A steel tank of the Purity Distilling Co. (owned by US Industrial Alcohol), 50 ft tall and 90 ft wide.
- It held about 2.3 million US gallons (8,700 m³), roughly 12,000 tonnes.
- The tank leaked from the start. The company painted it brown to hide the leaks, and local people collected the drips.
- The day before it was about 2 °F (−17 °C). That day it went above 40 °F (4 °C).
- Witnesses heard a sound like machine-gun fire as the rivets shot out.
- The wave was up to 25 ft (7.6 m) high and moved at about 35 mph (56 km/h). It bent the girders of the elevated railway and pushed buildings off their foundations.
- Molasses is shear-thinning: it gets runnier when pushed. In the evening it cooled and thickened, which trapped victims and slowed rescuers.
- 21 people died and about 150 were injured. The court found USIA liable. Afterwards Boston, and then other places, required engineers and architects to sign and file their calculations.
- The harbour stayed brown until summer. For decades, locals said the area smelled of molasses on hot days.

## Script (~205 words, ~57 s at 3.7 words/s)
1. HOOK: In 1919, a wave of syrup flattened part of Boston.
2. SETTING: January fifteenth. The North End. A steel tank, fifty feet tall, holds two point three million gallons of molasses.
3. ACTION 1: The tank leaks. So much that the company paints it brown, so the leaks won't show.
4. ACTION 2: Neighbours bring buckets to catch the free drips.
5. ACTION 3: That week the city is freezing. Then, one morning, it warms up to forty degrees.
6. SNAP: Just after noon, the rivets pop like gunfire, and the tank splits open.
7. WAVE: A wall of molasses, twenty-five feet high, rolls down the street at thirty-five miles an hour. It bends the railway above. It shoves houses off their foundations.
8. DEADPAN: Luckily, molasses is slow. Unluckily, not when there are twelve thousand tons of it.
9. EXPLANATION: It's like ketchup in a bottle. Sitting still, it's thick. Push it hard, and it runs. Then the evening cooled it, and it set like honey from the fridge, holding people in place.
10. CONSEQUENCE: Twenty-one people died. The court blamed the company, and engineers were made to sign their calculations before anything got built.
11. CALLBACK: The harbour stayed brown until summer. And locals said that, on hot days, the North End still smelled sweet.
